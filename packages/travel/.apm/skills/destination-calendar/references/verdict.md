# Verdict

Findings are not an answer. The traveller needs a decision and the reasoning
behind it, in that order.

## Six effect axes

Score each finding on the axes it actually touches. Most touch two or three,
and conflating them produces the useless "it will be busy" non-answer.

| Axis | The question |
|---|---|
| Price | Do rooms and fares cost materially more? |
| Availability | Does supply run out, and how far ahead? |
| Crowding | Are the places the traveller wants to be full? |
| Closures | Is anything shut, or on altered hours? |
| Transport | Are roads, rail or air disrupted? |
| Atmosphere | Is the city better or worse to be in? |

Atmosphere is the axis that flips sign. Everything else is a cost; this one can
be the entire reason to go.

## The four verdicts

### Shift the dates

When the gain from moving beats the cost of moving. Give the specific
alternative window and what it saves — "the week after, once the congress
clears, halves the room rate" — not a vague suggestion to avoid peak season.

Check the alternative window too. Moving into a different event is the classic
failure.

### Book now

When the dates are fixed and supply is going. Say what to book first and by
when: the constrained thing is usually accommodation, sometimes a specific
train, occasionally a timed-entry ticket.

This is also the verdict when the finding is mild but the lead time is short.

### Fine, plan around it

When the effect is real but bounded — one district, one morning, one closed
museum. Name the specific accommodation: stay outside the affected area, do
the closed thing on a different day, allow extra time on the marathon morning.

Most findings land here. Resist inflating them.

### Go because of it

When the event is worth arranging the trip around. Say what it costs: book
much earlier, pay more, accept crowds. A recommendation that hides the cost is
not a recommendation.

## Writing the answer

1. **The verdict, in one line.** The reader may stop here.
2. **What drove it** — the one or two findings that decided it, with dates.
3. **Everything else found**, briefly, so nothing is hidden.
4. **What was not checked.** The boundary of the search.

## Weighing findings

- A finding that touches **price and availability together** is the strongest
  signal, because it cannot be planned around — only paid for or moved away
  from.
- **Closures** matter in proportion to how central the closed thing is to the
  trip. A shut museum is fatal to a museum trip and irrelevant to a walking
  one.
- **Transport disruption** compounds: it does not just cost its own time, it
  puts every connection at risk.
- **Several mild findings on the same dates** are not mild. A school holiday
  plus a bridge day plus a mid-size conference is a full city.

## The honest non-answer

When the sources could not be reached, or coverage is absent for the
destination, say so plainly and give the verdict as unknown. A confident
verdict on an incomplete check is the worst output this skill can produce — it
is the exact failure the skill exists to prevent.
