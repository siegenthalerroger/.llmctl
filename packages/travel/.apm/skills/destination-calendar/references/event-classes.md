# Event Classes

The eight classes of thing that wreck a travel window. No keyless API ranks them, so each has to be ruled in or out by hand. Work the list in order — it runs roughly from most to least damaging per occurrence.

Examples are illustrative, not a lookup table. Dates rotate, host cities rotate, and an example that was right last year is a trap this year.

## 1. Trade fairs and congresses

The single largest hidden driver of hotel prices, and the one travellers never think to check. A mid-size congress can triple room rates across a whole city while leaving every restaurant, museum and train exactly as it was — so the symptom is "why is this hotel €600" with no visible cause.

Signals: Messe cities (Frankfurt, Hannover, Düsseldorf, Cologne, Munich), Barcelona in late winter, Milan in April, Las Vegas in January, Geneva, Basel, Singapore. Any city with a purpose-built convention centre has a calendar; find it.

Effect: **price and availability, severely.** Little else.

## 2. Major sport

Fixed-venue motorsport weekends (Silverstone, Monza, Spa, Monaco), championship finals, tournament rounds, and anything that fills a stadium in a small town. The surrounding area books out months ahead and rates hold even for the nights either side.

Marathons are a separate case: little effect on price, large effect on movement — road closures across the centre for a morning, and metro pressure all day.

Effect: price and availability for the big ones; **transport and closures** for city-centre races.

## 3. Festivals

Music, arts and film. Glastonbury, Edinburgh in August (the Fringe roughly doubles the city's population), Oktoberfest, Sónar, Carnival, Sanremo, Venice Biennale and film festival.

The large ones distort an entire region, not just the host city — Edinburgh in August pushes demand into Glasgow.

Effect: everything, and often positively if the festival is why you are going.

## 4. Public and school holidays

The script covers these; they are listed here so the class is not forgotten when reading a verdict. The parts worth restating:

- **School holidays move regionally.** German Länder, Swiss cantons and French zones stagger deliberately so the whole country is not away at once.
- **Bridge days multiply a single holiday** into a four-day weekend.
- **Some are effectively national shutdowns**: Ferragosto in Italy, most of Paris in August, Golden Week and Obon in Japan, Lunar New Year across much of Asia. Businesses close for the week, not the day.

Effect: price, crowding, and closures.

## 5. Religious observance

Ramadan (daytime closures, altered opening hours, transformed evenings), Eid, Semana Santa, Yom Kippur (in Israel, near-total shutdown including the airport), Passover, Diwali, Easter across Catholic and Orthodox calendars — which fall on different dates.

Frequently invisible in the holiday table when the country is not majority observant but the destination neighbourhood is.

Effect: **closures and opening hours**, atmosphere, occasionally transport.

## 6. Political and institutional events

Summits and set-piece weeks: WEF in Davos, COP wherever it lands, UN General Assembly week in New York, party conferences in UK seaside towns, NATO and G7 meetings.

These bring security perimeters and road closures on top of the price effect, and they are announced with less lead time than a trade fair.

Effect: price, availability, **closures and security disruption**.

## 7. Commemorative and civil-rights dates

Pride weeks and marches, Stonewall and other movement anniversaries, national days, liberation and remembrance days, and round-number anniversaries of historic events — which draw far larger crowds than the same date in an ordinary year.

Worth checking the year specifically: a 50th or 60th anniversary is a different event from the 49th.

Effect: crowding and atmosphere; price where the city is a focal destination for it.

## 8. Civic disruption

Rail and air strikes (France, Germany, Italy, Belgium and the UK all run recurring disputes), planned engineering closures, air traffic control action, and demonstrations.

Announced on short notice, so this is the one class worth re-checking close to departure rather than only at planning time.

Effect: **transport**, and cascading into everything that depends on it.
