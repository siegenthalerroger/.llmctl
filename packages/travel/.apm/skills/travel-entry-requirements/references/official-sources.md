# Official Sources

Find the government page, not a summary of it. This file gives the shape of
the search rather than a link table, because URLs rot and a stale link is worse
than no link — it looks authoritative and is not.

## The two that are always needed

**The destination's own immigration or foreign ministry.** Authoritative on
what that state will admit. Search for the government domain specifically:
`site:.gov`, `site:.gov.uk`, `site:.gc.ca`, `site:europa.eu`, `site:.admin.ch`,
`site:.govt.nz`, and the equivalent national suffix elsewhere.

**The traveller's own foreign ministry.** Authoritative on advisories, and it
usually states the destination's requirements for that nationality — which is
exactly the cut the destination's own page often does not make.

Common ones: the UK FCDO travel advice pages, the US State Department country
information pages, Global Affairs Canada, the Australian Smartraveller service,
the German Auswärtiges Amt, the French Conseils aux Voyageurs, and the Swiss
EDA Reisehinweise.

## Schemes worth checking by name

Electronic authorisation schemes are where remembered answers go most wrong,
because launch dates slip repeatedly and coverage is phased. Check the scheme's
own official page for whether it is in force **for this nationality on this
date**, never a news article about it:

- The EU's ETIAS authorisation and its EES biometric border system
- The UK's ETA
- The US ESTA
- Canada's eTA
- Equivalents in Australia, New Zealand, and a growing list elsewhere

"Announced", "adopted" and "in force" are three different states. Only the
third binds a traveller.

## Health

The WHO's International Travel and Health guidance for vaccination
requirements, and the destination's own health ministry for anything current.
National travel-health services — the UK's NaTHNaC and TravelHealthPro, the
US CDC's travel pages — carry per-country advice for their own residents.

Distinguish **required** from **recommended**. Yellow fever certificates are
required for entry from certain countries and refused boarding follows without
one; most other vaccinations are recommendations.

## Customs

The destination's customs authority for import allowances and prohibited goods,
and the cash declaration threshold — commonly €10,000 or the local equivalent,
applied on entering and leaving.

## How to search

Put the nationality in the query, because the answer depends on it:
`<nationality> citizens visa requirements <destination> site:<gov domain>`.

Then confirm on the destination's page rather than stopping at the first
result. Aggregators rank well and update slowly.

## What not to cite

Airline help pages, OTA summaries, visa-agency sites that sell a service,
travel blogs, and forum answers. Each may point at the right rule; none is the
rule. A visa-agency page in particular has a commercial interest in the answer.
