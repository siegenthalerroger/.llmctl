# Intake

One batch of questions, asked once. Skip anything the request already answered — re-asking is the fastest way to look like a form rather than a planner.

Stop at what changes the plan. Everything below earns its place by altering an outcome; nothing here is collected for completeness.

## Always

**Who is going.** Number of travellers, ages if children, and any mobility, dietary or medical needs. This changes accommodation, pacing and often the destination.

**Which passports.** Nationality per traveller, and — where it might bind — passport expiry. This drives the whole entry-requirements branch, and a mixed-nationality party can face entirely different rules on the same trip.

**Dates, and how far they flex.** The crucial part is the flex. "Second week of June, could move a week either way" makes the timing check actionable; "June" does not, and "these exact dates" changes the verdict language.

**Origin.** Where the journey starts, which is not always home.

**Budget.** A range and what it covers — per person or total, flights included or not. Also whether it is a ceiling or a target.

## Usually

**What the trip is for.** Rest, sightseeing, an event, family, an anniversary, work plus leisure. Two trips with identical constraints and different purposes should not produce the same plan.

**Pace.** How much per day, and the tolerance for early starts and moving between places. Under-asked, and the largest cause of an itinerary that looks good and is exhausting.

**Been there before.** Changes everything about what to recommend — a second visit skips the headline sights.

**Accommodation preferences.** Hotel, apartment, or either. Central and small versus further out and larger.

**Non-negotiables.** The one thing that must happen, and the one thing that must not.

## When relevant

- Loyalty programmes and airline or hotel preferences
- Travel insurance already held
- Whether a car is wanted, and who can drive
- Dietary requirements that constrain a destination rather than a meal
- Anything already booked

## Asking well

- **Batch it.** One message, grouped, with the optional parts marked optional.
- **Propose defaults.** "I'll assume mid-range hotels, central, unless you say otherwise" is faster than a question and just as correctable.
- **Do not ask what you can infer.** A request for a beach week in August with two small children has answered several of these already.
- **Ask the flex question properly.** It is the one that determines whether the timing check can change anything, and a vague answer wastes the gate.
