# Deliverable

The plan is read twice: once to decide, and again on the ground to check a detail. Build it so both readings work from the same page, without a second request.

## Verdict and evidence, visible together

Every section that answers a question — timing, entry requirements, safety — opens with its verdict and keeps the facts behind it on screen.

- **The verdict** is one plain sentence with a text status: good, act, blocked or unknown. Where the format supports colour, reinforce the status with green, amber, red or neutral styling. An incomplete check stays unknown, with the missing evidence and next action visible; it never becomes a reassuring yes. Example: "Unknown — entry requirements still need checking for the traveller's passport and dates."
- **The facts** sit directly beneath as short rows, one finding per row: a status marker (good / watch / info / unknown), a bold lead term, and one line on what it means for this traveller. Example shape: "**[Verified holiday and date]** — [verified service closure and its consequence]."
- **Sources** go in one small line at the end of the section, or in a collapsible list at the foot of the page. Citations may collapse; findings never do.

A verdict alone forces the traveller to ask why; findings alone force them to work out the verdict. The page carries both.

## Options as comparable cards

Render flight and hotel options as comparable cards with the same fields in the same order. Place them side by side on wide screens and stack them on phones; use matching blocks or a table in a document:

1. A label naming the trade-off — "Fastest", "Best value", "Most miles".
2. The option's name.
3. The price, in the currency it was quoted in, with the tool that produced it.
4. One line of consequence — "lands 00:25, tight for a morning meeting".
5. The loyalty programme it earns, when points are a stated priority.

## Plain words

Short sentences. Name the consequence rather than the mechanism: "take a taxi — the airport train is rerouted" instead of a paragraph on the rail works. Keep every finding; cut the padding around it.

## Format

Use the traveller's requested format. Otherwise, prefer a web page when publishing is available and the traveller has authorised its audience and contents. Booking authority does not grant publishing authority. Prepare a local preview or document when sharing authority is absent or publishing is unavailable; ask only for the missing publishing decision if needed.

Keep confirmation references, booking-management links and personal or emergency-contact details in the traveller's private copy. A public page is a redacted version of that plan. Verify access controls before calling a hosted copy private; an unlisted URL alone is not private access.

Render the reservation tracker as a checklist with each item's computed date and current reservation state. A tick records a completed task, not evidence of a booking. If an interactive checklist is provided, verify whether its state survives a reload and say whether updates are local or shared.

State the booking-authority mode in force at the top of the page.

## Contents

The plan, the reservation tracker, confirmation references, emergency contacts, and the dated list of what to re-check before departure.
