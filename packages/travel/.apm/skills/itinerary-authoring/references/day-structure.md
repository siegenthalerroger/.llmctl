# Day Structure

The form every day entry takes, and why each part is there.

## The form

```
### Day 3 — Thursday 25 June — Lisbon

Anchor      Gulbenkian Museum, late morning. Quiet on weekdays, and the
            collection is small enough to do properly in two hours.
Backup      Time Out Market and the riverfront walk — indoor start, works
            in rain, no booking needed.
Nearby      Parque Eduardo VII; the Amoreiras viewpoint if the light is good.
Eat         Dinner booked, Taberna da Rua das Flores, 20:00. Books out
            about three weeks ahead.
Move        None. Third night at the same hotel.
Note        Museum closed Tuesdays — this is why the anchor sits on Thursday.
```

## Why each line exists

**Anchor** — one thing, named, with a reason. The reason is what lets the
traveller substitute something they would prefer; without it they either follow
the plan blindly or discard it.

**Backup** — the failure plan, decided in advance rather than at the moment of
failure, when nobody makes good decisions. It should differ from the anchor in
the way most likely to matter: indoors if the anchor is outdoors, unbooked if
the anchor needs booking, nearby if the anchor needs travel.

**Nearby** — options without times. This is where a day gets its slack. A
traveller who finishes early wants a suggestion, not a gap.

**Eat** — only where it needs a booking or where the area is genuinely thin.
Listing three meals a day is filler.

**Move** — changes of accommodation or city, and nothing else. Silence here
means the traveller wakes up where they went to sleep.

**Note** — the constraint that shaped the day. Closed days, a holiday found by
the timing check, a booking that had to be a specific date. This is what stops
someone helpfully rearranging the itinerary into a broken one.

## Sequencing days

- **Arrival day holds nothing.** Getting there, finding the place, eating
  nearby. Anything more is optimism.
- **Book the constrained day first.** A timed-entry ticket or a hard-to-get
  table fixes its day; build the rest around it.
- **Alternate intensity.** A long day followed by a light one. Two long days
  together are paid for on the third.
- **Departure day holds nothing either**, beyond a short thing near the
  accommodation if the flight is late.
- **Put the weather-dependent anchor early** in the trip where the plan allows,
  so there is a later day to move it to.

## Ordering within a day

Morning is for whatever is worst when crowded, and for anything with a queue.
Afternoon absorbs overruns. Evening is for things that do not depend on
opening hours.

Where the day involves travel between areas, group by geography rather than by
interest — two things on the same side of a city beat two better things at
opposite ends.

## What not to write

- Invented times on untimed things.
- More than two anchors, however tempting the destination.
- "Explore the old town" as a day's content. Name a route or a reason.
- Distances without durations. Three kilometres is meaningless; forty minutes
  uphill is a decision.
