# Lead Times

The booking windows that actually bite, and how to turn each into a date.

These are patterns, not a database. Confirm the specific window for the specific thing — venues change their policies — but knowing the shape tells you what to check and roughly when.

## How to compute a deadline

Work backwards from the release window, not forwards from today.

1. Find when booking **opens** for the date in question.
2. Find how fast it goes. "Sells out" and "usually available" are different deadlines.
3. Put a real date in the tracker, with a margin.

A thing that opens exactly 30 days ahead and sells out in hours has a deadline of *the day it opens*, which may be months from now — that belongs in the tracker as a dated action, not as a vague later concern.

## The patterns

**Timed-entry attractions.** The headline sights — a handful in most cities — release on a fixed horizon and sell out for peak dates. Some release in batches, which means a sold-out screen today is not final. Check whether a second release exists before advising a change of plan.

**Restaurants.** Commonly open 30 days ahead, sometimes at a specific hour, and the sought-after ones go within minutes. A booking platform's window is a property of the restaurant, not the platform. For anything with a reputation, the deadline is the release moment.

**Rail.** Advance fares open on a fixed horizon — often around three months in Europe — and the cheap buckets go first. Unlike flights, the walk-up fare is usually still available, so this is a cost deadline rather than an availability one, except on peak dates where it is both.

**Flights.** No single window. The pattern that matters is that prices rise into departure and availability in cheap fare classes disappears first. For a window the timing check flagged, book earlier than instinct suggests.

**Accommodation in a flagged window.** Where the timing check found a fair, race or festival, the deadline is now, and the right instrument is a refundable rate rather than the cheapest one.

**Tours and day trips.** Small-group ones cap out; large-bus ones rarely do. Anything with a single daily departure is effectively capped.

**Car hire.** Prices rise into the date and specific categories run out, particularly automatics in Europe and anything larger than compact in summer.

**Permits and regulated access.** Some trails, parks and sites run ballots or fixed-date releases months ahead, with no late availability at all. These are the ones that silently remove an itinerary item if missed, so check for them early wherever the trip has an outdoor centrepiece.

## In the tracker

Every row needs: what it is, where to book it, the computed date, the cost, and the current state. Sort by date.

Two things people get wrong:

- **Sorting by trip order.** The first thing to book is frequently the thing that happens last.
- **Recording "book early".** It is not a deadline, and it will be missed.

## Re-check before departure

Confirm the bookings exist, the times have not moved, and nothing has been cancelled. Airlines reschedule, restaurants close, and attractions change hours seasonally. A confirmation reference from three months ago is evidence of a booking made, not of a booking still valid.
