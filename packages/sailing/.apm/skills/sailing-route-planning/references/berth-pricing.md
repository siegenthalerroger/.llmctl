# Berth Pricing

Price the berth for the boat in front of you. Never quote the rate for "a 12 m boat" from a table.

## Inputs

- Length overall, and how the operator measures it (LOA, or length band boundaries)
- Monohull or multihull, plus beam if the operator prices by area
- Number of people aboard, for any per-person tax or fee
- Arrival and departure, with the operator's check-out time
- Whether shore power is needed, metered or flat, and water

## Build the figure

1. **Base rate** for the length band, in the season the date falls in. Many operators drop rates after a fixed date in autumn; check that the band and season match.
2. **Hull surcharge.** Multihulls commonly pay a percentage on top or a flat multiple. Look for it explicitly, because a headline rate rarely includes it.
3. **Utilities**: power (flat per day or per kWh) and water.
4. **Per-person charges**, such as tourist tax, unless the charter already prepaid them.
5. **Stay rules.** Find when a paid day ends (often noon the next day), and whether a short daytime stop is cheaper. Some port authorities charge a reduced day fee in their other ports once a night has been paid in one of them. Look for such rules in the price list's notes.

Show the arithmetic in one line: `<length> m × <rate>/m × <hull factor> + <power> + <people> × <tax> = <total>`. That lets the skipper check it.

## When there is no price

- Operators that publish no transit prices: give the phone number and VHF channel and say "price on request". Don't estimate.
- Third-party tables: use them only with the table's year, and mark the figure unverified.
- Restaurant jetties and buoy fields: the terms may be "free with dinner" or a fixed buoy fee. Name the condition, including whether the whole crew must eat.

## Report

One price column in the comparison table, showing the total for this boat for the night, with a note per row naming the source and year. List unverified figures again under the table's "could not verify" line.
