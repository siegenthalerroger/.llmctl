# Life Aboard

Plans for who sleeps where, what gets eaten and who does what. Each one starts from the crew survey and the boat's layout, so read both first.

## Cabins

- Allocate couples first, then singles. Ask before pairing singles who don't know each other.
- Put snorers away from light sleepers, and use the saloon or forepeak as overflow if the skipper agrees.
- Put people prone to seasickness low and near the centre of the boat, or aft. Avoid bow cabins for them.
- Give the skipper a cabin with quick access to the cockpit and chart table.
- Check how many heads there are and which cabins share them. State the plan as a proposal with its reasons.

## Provisioning and cooking

- **Meal plan first, shopping list second.** Use crew numbers per day, with crew changes and planned restaurant evenings taken out, so the quantities follow.
- **Respect capacity**: fridge and freezer volume, dry storage, water, gas. Plan fresh food to run down before long gaps between shops.
- **Shops on the route**: check that the supermarkets near planned stops exist and are open in the season. Small islands often have little or nothing. Put staples on the first, big shop.
- **Cooking rota**: pairs cook in turn. Pair cooking and washing up so the same people don't do both every night. Mark allergies on every recipe that contains the allergen.
- **Quantities** in the shopping list are totals per shop, grouped by shop section, with a column for what is already aboard.

## Chores and roles

Rotate lines and fenders, dinghy and outboard, heads and holding-tank discipline, rubbish, water-tank checks, and the morning engine check. Give each person one role per day.

## Daily rhythm

Propose it, don't impose it. Cover departure time, the lunch or swim stop, arrival with time to spare before reception closes or the light goes, and showers and laundry on marina nights. Tie clothing and laundry quantities to this rhythm.

## Boat fund

Ask whether the skipper runs it digitally or in cash, and keep it out of the packing list unless asked. Check what the destination needs in cash: small ports, buoy fields and island shops may not take cards.

## Connectivity and power

- Check, don't assume, whether each crew member's roaming plan covers the destination. Plans from outside a roaming zone often exclude it.
- At anchor the boat runs on batteries. Name which nights have shore power so charging and high-draw devices fit around them.
