# Crew Documents

What each document contains, in the order the trip needs them. Presentation follows the skipper's existing documents.

## Crew survey

Settle three things before drafting: bareboat or skippered, one boat or several, and whether the crew all know each other. On a bareboat with the user as skipper, the survey is a safety input, not only a preference poll.

- **Safety and admin**: contact details and an emergency contact per person; exactly what the charter company's crew list asks for (check its form, which differs by operator and flag state); sailing and radio certificates the charter requires; health and medication as free text with severity; allergies separate from dislikes; swimming ability; seasickness history.
- **Logistics**: arrival and departure times and mode, which week, any crew change.
- **Galley**: diets, what each person offers to cook, breakfast and lunch preferences, drinks, how often to eat ashore.
- **Preferences**: one matrix of at most about eight rows, with a "not at all" column at the zero end. Rows cover marina versus anchorage nights, passage length, rest and town days, departure time, helming, water activities. Include a few light-hearted rows that still return planning data, and put "cook" next to "wash up".
- **Consent**: photos and social media.
- **Closing note**: personal data is deleted after the trip.

## Survey analysis

One page per week or crew. It shows crew numbers and diets, cooking offers by person, meal preferences with counts, drinks with a per-day consumption basis, seasickness and swimming flags, and preference results that change the plan. Each finding names the survey numbers behind it.

## Packing lists

- **Crew list**: boxes before the list for luggage type, a suggested daily rhythm, expected weather and what is already aboard; then at most three grouped sections, numbered continuously, with columns # / item / hint; one closing line instead of a "leave at home" section.
- **Captain list**: central gear, spares and paperwork, plus every point still to settle.
- **Leave out** anything the charter rules exclude (for example night-passage gear when night sailing is not permitted) and anything the survey already collects.

## Menu plan

One row per evening: the dish, who cooks it (from their survey offer), and how it adapts to each diet without a second meal. Mark ashore evenings, and put demanding dishes on anchorage or harbour evenings, not passage days. Also cover dishes in reserve, breakfast and lunch per crew, and a cooking and washing-up rota where whoever cooks doesn't wash up.

## Cooking plan

Per dish: the cooking team, the recipe scaled to the crew with its source, a diet note per restriction (which labels to check, what is served separately, who handles meat), and open questions to the cook. Also list offered dishes that didn't get a slot, and a short message ready to paste into the group chat.

## Shopping list

Per shop or week:

- Dinner ingredients consolidated across dishes, each with a "for" note naming the dishes.
- Breakfast, lunch and snacks, drinks (with the consumption basis), and non-food items.
- What is already aboard, with estimated quantities flagged.
- Which fresh items to use first.
- Where to shop near each planned stop, with distance and opening hours, and any local Sunday or holiday trading rules.

## Route plan

Per day:

- Date, distance and the leg with waypoints and sights.
- The weather line, with the source and run time.
- The night stop with its rating, shelter directions and an alternative.
- The evening meal (aboard or ashore) and who cooks.

Also include an overview per week (distance, legs, crew, restaurant nights, crew change), a "when to re-check what" list tied to dated decisions, bad-weather alternatives with shelter directions, and open reservations, tickets and provisioning.

## Day-one briefing

A checklist for the skipper.

1. **Before the crew boards.** Takeover with the base: defects recorded, filmed and dived. Check the crew list and documents, count and check life jackets, check expiry dates on safety gear, and note where the seacocks, main switch, gas shut-off, emergency tiller and first-aid kit are. Look at the weather for the first days, and assign berths.
2. **Welcome and ground rules**: how decisions are made, and one hand for the boat.
3. **Tours.** Below deck: gas, extinguishers, seacocks, bilge pumps, heads, water. On deck: life jackets fitted by each person, jacklines, liferaft, MOB gear.
4. **Pinch and crush points**: winches, loaded lines, the boom, the anchor windlass, fenders.
5. **Lines, winches and commands.**
6. **Man overboard and emergencies**: local emergency numbers and VHF channels (looked up for the area), and a MAYDAY script at the radio.
7. **Rules for daily life**, and the drills for the first hours on the water.

## Daily plan poster

A printed poster aboard, filled in by hand each day.

- **Header**: weekday and date.
- **The day's leg**: from and to, earliest departure and latest arrival, distance in nm, time underway, wind direction and speed, waves.
- **Safety and warnings**: when life jackets are worn, the reef plan, visibility, anything special.
- **Day schedule**: time and what happens (manoeuvre, swim stop, shore leave, meals, back aboard). Plans change, and the poster says so.
- **Today's roles**: day skipper, navigator, deckhands, cooks.
- **Meals**: lunch and dinner, aboard or ashore.
- **Tomorrow**: destination, cooks, wake-up time.
- **Questions and notes**: a free field anyone may write in.
- **Fixed emergency panel**: the first MOB steps, which everyone can do, and where the life jackets, liferaft, extinguishers and first aid are.

## Feedback survey

Keep to the skipper's brief: what people enjoyed, whether they would come again (with the destination, year and duration options the skipper names), and what to do more or less of. Present any extra questions as proposals.

- **Favourite stop**: offer the list of actual overnight and day stops from the track log (the sailing-route-planning skill extracts them) instead of free text.
- **Name field**: if returning crew must be identifiable, the name cannot be optional. Raise the conflict instead of choosing silently.
