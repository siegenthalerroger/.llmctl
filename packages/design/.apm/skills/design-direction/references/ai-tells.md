# AI Design Tells, by Medium

An inventory of the choices that currently read as machine-generated. Read the section for the medium you are working in — the tells differ sharply between them, and applying a screen list to a print job produces worse work, not safer work.

## How to use this list

**Nothing here is bad design.** Every entry is a choice that works, has worked repeatedly, and has therefore stopped carrying information. A tell is a signal that a decision was skipped — the reader infers, correctly, that nobody chose.

Three rules govern its use:

1. **A stated preference outranks the entire list.** When someone asks for a gradient hero or a warm-cream editorial page, build it well. Precedence level 1 is absolute.
2. **Each entry names an underlying failure.** The specific look rotates; the failure does not. Fix the failure, not the surface.
3. **Do not invert the list.** Avoiding a tell by reaching for its opposite produces a different default. The answer to a purple gradient is a derived palette, not a green gradient.

## Cross-medium

- **Uniform density** — every section the same length, weight and treatment. *Failure:* no judgement was applied about what matters most; real documents have peaks and troughs.
- **The rule of three** — three columns, three bullets, three benefits, whatever the actual count. *Failure:* the structure was chosen before the content was counted.
- **Emoji as structural markers** — section headings, bullet glyphs, ✅/❌ as decoration. *Failure:* typographic hierarchy replaced by icon novelty.
- **Decoration shaped like structure** — numbered markers (01 / 02 / 03) on things that are not a sequence, dividers between things that are not separate, eyebrows that name no category. *Failure:* the reader is told something structural that is not true.
- **Centred by default** — sustained centred text gives the eye no left edge to return to. Centring is for short display lines.
- **Gradient standing in for hierarchy** — a colour transition used where size, weight or space should have done the work.
- **Invented poetic naming** for functional things — palette names, section labels, tier names — where a plain functional name would serve.

## Screen and web

- **The violet-to-blue gradient hero** on white, often with one headline word gradient-filled.
- **The warm-cream editorial preset** — a `#F4F1EA`-ish ground, serif display face, terracotta or rust accent. Now as templated as the gradient it replaced.
- **The single-pop dark page** — near-black ground with exactly one acid-green or vermilion accent.
- **Default-safe typefaces** — Inter, Space Grotesk, Poppins, Manrope chosen because they are safe rather than because they suit the subject.
- **Glassmorphism cards** floating over blurred gradient blobs.
- **One border-radius everywhere**, usually a mid rounding applied to cards, buttons, inputs and images alike. *Failure:* radius is a material property; a photo and a button are not the same material.
- **Accent bar or rail on every card**, applied as a set rather than to mark anything.
- **The full-viewport hero** — centred headline, subhead, solid primary button beside a ghost secondary.
- **The three-up feature grid** — icon over title over paragraph, three across, all at equal weight. *Failure:* equal visual weight asserts equal importance, which is almost never true.
- **Bento grids** used because the form is current, not because the content has genuinely different weights.
- **Icon-per-item as a completeness ritual** — every list item gets a glyph whether or not it clarifies anything.
- **Elevation on things that do not lift** — drop shadows applied as texture rather than to signal layering.
- **Fabricated social proof** — "trusted by" logo rows, invented testimonials, made-up metrics. This is a correctness failure before it is a design one; never generate it.

## Slide decks

- **Topic titles instead of assertions** — a slide headed "Q3 Revenue" whose actual point is "Revenue grew on retention, not new logos". *Failure:* the title names the subject rather than delivering the finding, so the audience must derive it. The single highest-leverage fix in deck design.
- **A thin accent rule under every title**, on every slide.
- **Every slide a bulleted list** at the same rhythm, usually three bullets.
- **An icon beside every bullet.**
- **The agenda slide** that restates the deck's own structure back to the audience.
- **The "Thank you / Questions?" terminal slide** — spends the most-remembered position in the deck on nothing.
- **Full-bleed stock photo, dark scrim, centred white text.**
- **Uniform slide density** — a deck with no rhythm cannot signal what is important. Some slides should carry one number.
- **Body text sized for reading rather than for the back of the room.**
- **Screen-tuned colour sent to a projector** — projection has far lower effective contrast and ambient light lifts the blacks. Low-contrast pairs and dark-on-dark subtleties that pass on a monitor disappear on a wall.

## Print and editorial

- **Justified text without hyphenation** — produces rivers. Justification only works with hyphenation enabled.
- **One measure for every text block** regardless of type size. *Failure:* measure is a function of size; a caption and body text at the same width are not both correct.
- **A drop cap on every section** rather than at genuine openings.
- **Hairline rules and dense multi-column setting** worn as an "editorial" costume by content that is not editorial.
- **Symmetric margins** on a bound or trimmed page, where asymmetric margins let the spread read as one unit.
- **Pull quotes repeating adjacent body text verbatim** — spends prime space on nothing new.
- **Widows and orphans left standing.**
- **Screen colour values sent to print** with no consideration of gamut — saturated screen blues and greens have no print equivalent.

## Identity and brand

- **The gradient-filled shield, hexagon, or abstract swoosh.**
- **Geometric sans wordmark, tracking slightly tightened, one letter in the accent colour.**
- **Overlapping translucent circles.**
- **A mark that only resolves at hero size.** *Failure:* no test at favicon size, in one colour, embroidered, or engraved. A mark that survives only in its presentation rendering is not an identity.
- **Retrofitted meaning** — "the negative space forms an arrow representing growth" attached to a shape that was chosen first. The rationale is written to justify the mark rather than the mark derived from the rationale.
- **A mark and wordmark that cannot work apart.**
- **A palette with poetic names and no functional roles** — no rule for what is primary, what is semantic, what is background.
- **A "brand system" that is one logo and three colours** with no rules for applying them across media. A system is the rules, not the assets.

## Data graphics

- **Gradients, drop shadows and 3D on marks** — none of it encodes anything, all of it distorts the quantity being read.
- **Every series a different fully saturated hue at the same lightness.** *Failure:* the series collide under colour-vision deficiency and vanish in greyscale, because only hue was varied.
- **A legend where direct labelling fits** — costs the reader a round trip for every value read.
- **Dashboard chrome around three numbers.**
- **Chart type chosen for variety** rather than for the comparison being made.
- **Truncated or absent baselines on bars** — bar length *is* the encoding, so a non-zero baseline misstates the data.
- **Decorative gridlines heavier than the data.**
- **No source, no date, no units.** *Failure:* a chart is a claim; an unattributed claim cannot be checked.

## Copy

Words are design material, and they carry their own tells:

- **Headline abstractions** — seamless, effortless, unlock, elevate, transform, supercharge, leverage.
- **Antithesis rhythm** — "It's not X — it's Y", repeated as a structural tic.
- **System-side naming** — "Webhook configuration" where the reader thinks "Notifications". Name things as the reader recognises them.
- **Controls that do not say what happens** — "Get started", "Learn more". A button says the action; the confirmation echoes it in the past tense.
- **Placeholder-shaped specifics** — "10,000+ teams", "99.9% faster". Invented numbers are a correctness failure; never generate them.

## Why this list rots, and what does not

The surfaces rotate. The warm-cream editorial look was the *correction* to the purple gradient two years ago and is now the same kind of default. Anything specific here has a shelf life, and this list was written in **August 2026**.

The underlying failures do not rotate:

- A decision was skipped and a default filled the gap.
- Visual weight asserts an importance the content does not have.
- A structural device encodes nothing true.
- A form was chosen before the content was understood.
- A choice was tested only in the medium it was authored in.

When a specific entry here looks dated, discard it and check the failure instead.
